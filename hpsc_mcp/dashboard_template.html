What this is

      
Drug companies in Canada must report every drug shortage and discontinuation to Health Canada's reporting site, Health Product Shortages Canada. The data is valuable, but many reports are never closed or updated. This monitor rebuilds the list every day, merges duplicate reports for the same product, and labels each report by how fresh its information is.

      
Report freshness labels

      

        
Updated in last 14 days
The company changed the report in the last 14 days, and the end date it gave has not passed.

        
End date still ahead
The estimated end date is in the future; no change in the last 14 days.

        
No end date given
The company has not given an estimated end date (and has updated the report within the last year).

        
No update in 12+ months
The report is still open and has not been changed in over a year.

        
Past estimated end date
The report is still open, but the end date the company gave has already passed.

      

      
Companies must update a report within 2 calendar days of any change, and post within 2 days of being able to meet demand again (Health Canada). A report can be correct and still unchanged for a long time, so the last two labels are prompts to confirm with your supplier, not findings of fault.

      
Same-ingredient products

      
For every product with an open report, the monitor lists the other marketed products with the same active ingredient(s), strength, dosage form and route, taken from Health Canada's Drug Product Database, and shows which of them also have an open shortage report. Ingredients are matched on the base name, so makers using different salts of the same base are grouped together.

      
Beside each short product, the label counts only the other products in its group:

      

        
Green: "3 other products, no open report"
At least one other marketed product with the same ingredient, strength, form and route has no open shortage report.

        
Amber: "... · check presentation"
The same, but the group is injectable, inhaled, a patch or a biologic (Schedule D). These can differ in presentation (vial, pen, cartridge, device) or be a different brand that is not a straight swap.

        
Red: "All 3 others also short" or "No other marketed product"
Every marketed product in the group has an open report, or this is the only marketed product of its kind.

        
Grey: "Not on marketed list"
The DIN is not in Health Canada's list of marketed human products, so no comparison is made. The product may no longer be marketed, or it is a veterinary or radiopharmaceutical product.

      

      
"No open report" is not a stock check. A maker without a report can still be on allocation or out at your wholesaler, and an open report can be out of date (see the freshness label). A same-ingredient match is not a statement that two products are interchangeable; that depends on your provincial formulary and the patient. This monitor does not suggest a different drug as a therapeutic alternative.

      
Supply risk

      
The Supply risk view lists every marketed drug, strength, form and route with only one or two makers, from Health Canada's Drug Product Database, and shows which of them have an open shortage report and how many of their reports were marked resolved in the past 12 months. It describes the market. It is not a clinical ranking, and a drug with one maker is not necessarily at risk of running out.

      
Worksheets and patient handouts

      
Each product with an open report has a printable pharmacist worksheet and a patient handout. The worksheet lists the same-ingredient products, the other strengths and forms of the same ingredient, and the other marketed drugs that share its ATC class code, each with its shortage status. The class list is a starting point for a pharmacist's own review. It is not a recommendation, and this monitor never suggests switching a patient to a different drug. The patient handout repeats what the company reported, in plain language, with general guidance only.

      
Weekly brief

      
The brief covers the last 7 days and is rebuilt every day. "New" means the company created the report in that period. It is also published as a plain text file at the same address each day.

      
Today

      
The Today view lists only the reports that are new or were changed in the chosen window (since your last visit, since yesterday, or in the last 7 days), split into those with no other same-ingredient product free of a report, those where another maker has no open report, and reports marked resolved. Changed reports for products that are not on the marketed list are left out of it and counted in a footnote. "Since your last visit" uses a date saved in your browser.

      
Watchlist

      
The watchlist is stored only in your browser on your device. It is not sent to any server, so it does not follow you to another device or browser, and clearing your browser data removes it.

      
Method

      
Every open report (actual and anticipated) is pulled daily from the official public API, plus the 100 most recently resolved reports. Duplicates are merged by DIN; the most recently updated report leads each row. Every label states a date fact taken from the report itself; none is a judgement about the company or the shortage. Tier 3 is Health Canada's flag for the shortages with the greatest potential impact.

      
Limits

      
This is an independent project, not affiliated with Health Canada or the operator of the reporting site. It shows national manufacturer reports only, not local pharmacy stock. It is information only and not medical advice; never change a medication without your prescriber or pharmacist.
