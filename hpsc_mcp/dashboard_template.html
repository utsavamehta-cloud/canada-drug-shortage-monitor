What this is

Drug companies in Canada must report every drug shortage and discontinuation to Health Canada's reporting site, Health Product Shortages Canada. The data is valuable, but many reports are never closed or updated. This monitor rebuilds the list every day, merges duplicate reports for the same product, and labels each report by how fresh its information is.

Report freshness labels

Updated in last 14 days
The company changed the report in the last 14 days, and the end date it gave has not passed.
End date still ahead
The estimated end date is in the future; no change in the last 14 days.
No end date given
The company has not given an estimated end date (and has updated the report within the last year).
No update in 12+ months
The report is still open and has not been changed in over a year.
Past estimated end date
The report is still open, but the end date the company gave has already passed.

Companies must update a report within 2 calendar days of any change, and post within 2 days of being able to meet demand again (Health Canada). A report can be correct and still unchanged for a long time, so the last two labels are prompts to confirm with your supplier, not findings of fault.

Method

Every open report (actual and anticipated) is pulled daily from the official public API, plus the 100 most recently resolved reports. Duplicates are merged by DIN; the most recently updated report leads each row. Every label states a date fact taken from the report itself; none is a judgement about the company or the shortage. Tier 3 is Health Canada's flag for the shortages with the greatest potential impact.

Limits

This is an independent project, not affiliated with Health Canada or the operator of the reporting site. It shows national manufacturer reports only, not local pharmacy stock. It is information only and not medical advice; never change a medication without your prescriber or pharmacist.

Source: Health Product Shortages Canada public API. Independent project, not affiliated with Health Canada.
